Construction Accounting Case Study: Contractor Invoice Dispute

The Customer

A construction business became involved in a dispute with a contractor after receiving invoices that appeared to exceed the amount originally agreed upon. The contractor issued additional invoices and demanded payment beyond the client’s understanding of the original contract amount. When the client questioned the discrepancies and requested clarification, the situation escalated into a lawsuit.

The project involved contracts, change orders, progress payments, invoices, receipts, purchase records, and extensive supporting documentation. Without a structured reconciliation, it was difficult to determine exactly what was legitimately due, what had already been paid, and which amounts were unsupported or duplicated.

The Challenge

The client needed more than a simple review of their books. They needed a disciplined financial investigation that could connect invoices, payments, and project records into one verified timeline.

  • Questionable invoice totals: The contractor’s demand exceeded the client’s understanding of the original agreed amount, creating uncertainty around the financial position.
  • Multiple financial records: The project included invoices, payments, purchase records, receipts, and documentation that had to be reconciled against one another.
  • Duplicate or excess charges: The records needed to be reviewed to determine whether additional charges were tied to valid project work or represented repeated billing.
  • Unclear payment history: The client needed to know which amounts had already been paid and which remained outstanding.
  • Documentation gaps: Supported records had to be organized so discrepancies could be traced back to their source invoice, payment, or supporting document.
  • Potential lawsuit exposure: Without a reliable financial trail, the client had no clear basis to defend their position in a formal dispute.
Our Approach

AI Finora approached the project as a detailed invoice and payment reconciliation review. Rather than relying on the total balance claimed by the contractor, we examined the underlying documentation to compare actual project activity against the agreed contract amount.

  • Established the financial baseline: We identified the original amount agreed upon and used it as the reference point for reviewing subsequent invoices and payments.
  • Reviewed each invoice individually: We examined invoice amounts, dates, charges, supporting documentation, prior billings, and payment relationships to determine if the amounts were legitimate.
  • Reconciled payments against invoices: We matched outstanding charges to actual payments and supporting records so settled amounts were not treated as still due.
  • Identified excess and duplicate charges: We separated legitimate project-related items from unsupported, duplicated, or inflated charges so the discrepancy could be clearly explained.
  • Organized documentation: Relevant project records, invoices, payment history, and supporting files were structured into a cleaner evidence trail for the client and their advisors.
The Result

The reconciliation created a clear, defendable understanding of the project’s financial history and helped demonstrate where the contractor’s position did not match the underlying records.

  • Clear contract baseline: The client could clearly establish what had originally been agreed and compare it to all later charges.
  • Verified payment history: Payments were matched with invoices to identify amounts already settled and amounts that actually remained outstanding.
  • Excess charge identification: The review surfaced unsupported and duplicated amounts that were not properly supported by the accounting records.
  • Organized financial evidence: Relevant records were consolidated into a structured format that legal advisors could use in addressing the dispute.
  • Favorable legal outcome: The client ultimately won the case, supported by the clearer financial narrative and documentation produced through reconciliation.
The Summary

This case shows why contractor invoice disputes require more than a quick review of one balance sheet or one invoice total. When project records are incomplete or inconsistent, a business may face a claim that looks valid on paper but fails under detailed reconciliation. By reviewing each invoice, reconciling payment history, identifying duplicate or excess charges, and organizing supporting documentation, AI Finora helped the client build a clearer and stronger financial case.

  • Detailed invoice review for every project-related charge
  • Matching of invoice activity to payments and supporting documents
  • Identification of excess, unsupported, and duplicate charges
  • Structured documentation to support the client’s position
  • Client ultimately won the case
