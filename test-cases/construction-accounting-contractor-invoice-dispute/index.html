Construction Accounting Case Study: Contractor Invoice Dispute

How AI Finora Helped a Construction Business Uncover Excess Contractor Charges and Strengthen Its Financial Case

Industry: Construction & Contracting

Service: Construction Accounting & Financial Reconciliation

Challenge: Contractor Invoice Dispute and Lawsuit

Focus: Invoice Audit, Payment Reconciliation, Duplicate Charges & Documentation

Outcome: Client successfully resolved the dispute in their favor

The Customer

When Contractor Invoices Don't Match the Agreed Amount

Construction projects involve contracts, change orders, progress payments, invoices, receipts, purchase records, and a significant amount of financial documentation. When those records are not carefully reconciled, even a small discrepancy can turn into a serious financial dispute.

This case involved a construction business that became involved in a dispute with a contractor after receiving invoices that appeared to exceed the amount originally agreed upon.

The contractor issued additional invoices and demanded payment beyond the client's understanding of the agreed contract amount. When the client questioned the discrepancies and requested clarification, the situation escalated into a lawsuit.

At that point, the client needed more than a simple review of their books. They needed a structured financial investigation of the project records to determine exactly what had been invoiced, what had already been paid, what was legitimately due, and where the discrepancies occurred.

That's where AI Finora's construction accounting and reconciliation expertise became valuable.

The Challenge

Understanding What Was Actually Owed

The client's financial records contained multiple invoices, payments, and supporting documents related to the contractor and construction project.

The central questions were:

  • What amount was originally agreed upon?
  • Which invoices were legitimately associated with the project?
  • Which payments had already been made?
  • Were any charges duplicated?
  • Were additional invoices supported by appropriate documentation?
  • Did the total amount being demanded match the underlying financial records?
  • Could the client's payment history and supporting documents be clearly organized?

Without a complete reconciliation, it would have been difficult to establish a reliable financial picture of the dispute.

The challenge was therefore not simply finding one incorrect invoice. It was reconstructing the project's financial history and connecting invoices, payments, and supporting documentation into one clear record.

Our Approach

A Detailed Construction Accounting Audit

AI Finora approached the project as a detailed invoice and payment reconciliation exercise.

Rather than reviewing only the disputed invoices, we examined the available financial documentation individually and compared the records against the agreed amounts and payment history.

1. Reviewed the Original Financial Agreement

We first established the financial baseline for the project.

This meant identifying the amounts that were originally agreed upon and using those figures as the reference point for reviewing subsequent invoices and payments.

Having a clear baseline is critical in contractor disputes because every subsequent financial transaction needs to be evaluated against the underlying agreement and documented changes.

2. Audited Individual Contractor Invoices

We reviewed the contractor's invoices individually rather than relying on the total balance presented by the contractor.

The review focused on:

  • Invoice amounts
  • Invoice dates
  • Project-related charges
  • Previously billed amounts
  • Additional charges
  • Duplicate or potentially repeated items
  • Supporting documentation
  • Payment history
  • Relationship between invoices and agreed amounts

This helped create a clearer picture of how the contractor's claimed balance had been calculated.

3. Reconciled Invoices Against Payments

One of the most important parts of the process was comparing the contractor's invoices with the client's actual payment records.

We matched the available:

Invoices → Payments → Supporting Documents → Agreed Amounts

This reconciliation process helped identify which amounts had already been paid and prevented previously settled amounts from being treated as outstanding again.

For construction businesses, this type of accounts payable reconciliation can be particularly important when multiple invoices and project payments are involved.

4. Identified Excess and Duplicate Charges

During the review, we identified discrepancies between the amounts supported by the financial records and the additional amounts being demanded.

The analysis helped distinguish between:

Legitimate project-related amounts versus additional, unsupported, or duplicate charges.

Rather than simply reporting that the numbers did not match, we organized the findings so that each discrepancy could be traced back to the relevant invoice, payment, or supporting document.

This created a much clearer financial trail.

5. Organized Supporting Documentation

A strong financial reconciliation is much more useful when the underlying documentation is organized.

AI Finora helped structure the relevant records so that the client could more easily understand and present the financial history of the project.

The documentation included relevant:

  • Contractor invoices
  • Payment records
  • Accounting records
  • Supporting financial documents
  • Reconciliation findings
  • Amount comparisons

This transformed a collection of individual financial records into a more structured evidence trail.

From Individual Records to a Traceable Financial Position

The key objective was to establish a clear comparison between the agreed amount, amounts invoiced, amounts paid, and amounts actually supported by the records.

A reconciliation framework such as the following can help construction businesses analyze similar disputes:

What Construction Businesses Can Learn From This Case

Building Stronger Financial Controls Before a Dispute Happens

Contractor invoice disputes can become significantly more complicated when financial records are incomplete, scattered, or not reconciled regularly.

This case demonstrates why construction businesses should maintain strong project accounting and financial controls throughout the life of a project—not only when a dispute occurs.

1. Reconcile Contractor Invoices Regularly

Don't wait until a dispute happens.

Construction businesses should regularly compare contractor invoices against:

  • Contracts
  • Approved change orders
  • Purchase records
  • Payment records
  • Project budgets
  • Previous invoices

Regular reconciliation can help identify discrepancies before they become larger problems.

2. Keep a Complete Payment Trail

Every contractor payment should be traceable.

Businesses should retain documentation showing:

Who was paid → How much was paid → When it was paid → What invoice it relates to

A clear payment trail can make financial investigations significantly easier.

3. Don't Assume the Invoice Total Is Correct

An invoice represents a claim for payment. It should still be reviewed against the underlying agreement and project records.

This is particularly important when a contractor submits:

  • Additional invoices
  • Revised invoices
  • Change-order charges
  • Repeated charges
  • Adjusted balances
  • Previously disputed amounts

4. Document Change Orders Carefully

Construction projects frequently change.

Additional work can legitimately result in additional costs—but those changes should be properly documented and approved according to the applicable contract.

A strong change-order process can help prevent disagreements over whether an additional charge was authorized.

5. Maintain Construction Accounting Records Throughout the Project

Good construction accounting is not simply about recording income and expenses.

It can involve:

  • Job costing
  • Contractor payments
  • Accounts payable
  • Invoice management
  • Project budgets
  • Cost tracking
  • Payment reconciliation
  • Financial reporting
  • Documentation management

Keeping these records organized throughout the project can make it much easier to investigate a financial discrepancy later.

When Should You Consider an Invoice Audit?

Warning Signs That May Require a Financial Review

A detailed invoice audit may be appropriate when your business notices:

  • An invoice doesn't match the contract
  • The same expense appears more than once
  • A contractor submits unexpected additional charges
  • Previously paid amounts appear on a new statement
  • Project costs suddenly exceed expectations
  • Change-order amounts are unclear
  • Payment records don't match vendor statements
  • The outstanding balance cannot be independently verified

When these warning signs appear, a structured invoice reconciliation and accounting review can help establish what the financial records actually show.

For legal disputes, businesses should also work with qualified legal counsel. An accounting review can clarify the financial records, while attorneys determine the appropriate legal strategy.
